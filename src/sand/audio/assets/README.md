# Audio asset sources

These files are edited derivatives of recordings published under Creative
Commons Zero (CC0). Attribution is not required, but the sources are retained
here so the asset history stays auditable.

License: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/ (as displayed on
each linked Freesound page; source and license checked 2026-07-23).

Edits: trimmed, filtered, loudness-normalized, resampled, and (for the movement
beds) crossfaded into seamless mono loops. The TNT recordings were shortened,
level-matched, and faded so chain reactions retain space between individual
bursts. The gun recordings were taken from Freesound's high-quality previews,
trimmed to their useful transients, high-pass filtered, converted to mono, faded,
and encoded as 96 kbps MP3. “Rifle Burst” was extracted by its publisher from a
public-domain US military recording; the other two sources document their CC0
inputs or explicit CC0 redistribution permission on their linked pages.

The weapon mix follows the acoustic structure described by Robert C. Maher and
Steven R. Shaw: the muzzle blast is a very short chaotic impulse, while nearby
recordings can also expose the weapon's later mechanical action and overlapping
reflections. The mixer therefore layers the restrained recordings with a sharp
synthesized pressure crack, low body/reflection, and a delayed action transient:
https://www.montana.edu/rmaher/publications/maher_aesconf_0608_1-8.pdf
